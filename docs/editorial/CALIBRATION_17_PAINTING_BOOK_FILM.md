## Liberty Leading the People — Eugène Delacroix, 1830

### A

In July 1830, King Charles X restricted freedom of the press and changed the rules governing elections. The measures provoked three days of fighting in Paris. Insurgents forced him from power, and Louis-Philippe replaced him under a constitutional monarchy. Eugène Delacroix painted *Liberty Leading the People* later that year, giving the uprising a central figure who embodies its claim to freedom. [Histoire par l’image](https://histoire-image.org/etudes/liberte-guidant-peuple-eugene-delacroix)

The woman leading the crowd personifies Liberty. Her bare chest and draped clothing recall an ancient goddess, while her rifle places her in modern street fighting. She wears the red cap associated with the Revolution of 1789 and raises the tricolour, banned under the restored monarchy. These symbols connect the new uprising with earlier revolutionary demands. [Louvre](https://www.louvre.fr/en/explore/the-palace/think-big)

Around her, a worker in an apron, a man in a top hat and a boy carrying pistols advance together. Their differences help Delacroix present the people as a collective force. They cross a barricade strewn with corpses, while a wounded man struggles up toward Liberty. The bodies include soldiers killed on the opposing side. Delacroix’s celebration of the uprising retains the physical disorder and deaths of the fighting.

### B

Eugène Delacroix painted *Liberty Leading the People* after the July Revolution of 1830. Charles X’s restrictions on the press and Parliament had provoked three days of fighting in Paris. His overthrow brought Louis-Philippe to the throne. Delacroix made the armed crowd and its symbolic leader the subjects of his painting.

The woman carrying the tricolour is Liberty personified. Her bare chest and draped clothing recall an ancient goddess, but she carries a modern rifle and wears a revolutionary red cap. Around her are a worker in an apron, a man in a top hat and a boy with pistols. Their shared advance presents the revolt as a collective achievement. The corpses underfoot and wounded man beside her keep that achievement connected to the casualties of street fighting. [Histoire par l’image](https://histoire-image.org/etudes/liberte-guidant-peuple-eugene-delacroix)

This combination unsettled some critics when the painting appeared at the Paris Salon in 1831. They objected to the dirt and blood on its people, and to a Liberty who looked too much like a working woman. Delacroix made an established symbol of freedom look as though she belonged among the people fighting in the streets. [Panorama de l’art](https://panoramadelart.com/analyse/le-28-juillet-1830-la-liberte-guidant-le-peuple)

## Vidas Secas — Graciliano Ramos, 1938

### A

Graciliano Ramos’s novel *Vidas Secas*, published in 1938, follows a family searching for food and work in northeastern Brazil. Fabiano and Sinha Vitória travel with their two boys and their dog, Baleia. Drought threatens them, but finding work does not free them from poverty.

Fabiano tends another man’s cattle and depends on him for somewhere to live. Sinha Vitória’s calculations show that he should receive more than his employer pays. Fabiano protests, then backs down when the employer tells him to find work elsewhere. Correct arithmetic cannot protect them when the same man controls their pay and their home. [Zenir Campos Reis](https://www.scielo.br/j/ea/a/4P76p7GKXFft33XjbtKdLqk/?format=html&lang=pt)

Pleased that he has kept his family alive, Fabiano tells himself, “Fabiano, you’re a man.” He then corrects himself: “You’re an animal, Fabiano.” He knows his subordinate position, but the word also carries pride in his endurance. [Vidas Secas](https://brasiliana.usp.br/handle/bbm/8399)

The narration follows thoughts the family struggles to put into words. Sinha Vitória imagines a proper bed; the boys ask questions; Baleia seeks food and affection. They want rest, understanding and companionship, alongside enough food to survive. [Alcides Villaça](https://www.scielo.br/j/ea/a/nHJsJDx8TkxHMC6yyJbYbjL/)

### B

In the novel *Vidas Secas*, published in 1938, Graciliano Ramos follows a family displaced by drought in northeastern Brazil. Fabiano, Sinha Vitória, their two boys and their dog, Baleia, need food, work and a home. Hunger strains their affection. During the journey, Fabiano strikes his exhausted son, then takes pity and carries him.

The family’s vulnerability continues when they find work. Their employer controls the accounts and can dismiss Fabiano when he protests. Sinha Vitória calculates correctly, but that does not secure fair payment. Their poverty involves dependence on other people’s power as well as the weather. [Zenir Campos Reis](https://www.scielo.br/j/ea/a/4P76p7GKXFft33XjbtKdLqk/?format=html&lang=pt)

After a policeman provokes him, Fabiano is beaten and imprisoned. When a guard approaches, he says nothing is wrong. The narrator adds: “There were many things. He couldn’t explain them, but they were there.” Fear and limited speech conceal thoughts that the narration lets the reader follow. [Vidas Secas](https://brasiliana.usp.br/handle/bbm/8399)

Ramos also enters the thoughts of Sinha Vitória, the boys and Baleia. Their inner lives include curiosity, irritation, tenderness and ordinary wishes, such as Sinha Vitória’s hope for a proper bed. Their sparse conversations leave much unsaid; the changing viewpoints allow those separate experiences to become part of the family’s story. [Alcides Villaça](https://www.scielo.br/j/ea/a/nHJsJDx8TkxHMC6yyJbYbjL/)

## I’m Still Here — Walter Salles, 2024

### A

Walter Salles’s *I’m Still Here* (2024) follows the Paiva family in Rio de Janeiro under Brazil’s military dictatorship. Rubens, a former congressman, and Eunice raise five children in a home full of music, friends and political discussion. When agents take Rubens away in January 1971, the film follows the family left waiting for him. It is based on their son Marcelo’s memoir. [Sony Pictures Classics](https://www.sonyclassics.com/film/imstillhere/)

Eunice and one of her daughters are also detained. After her release, she must care for the children while trying to discover what has happened to their father. The authorities deny holding him. Their refusal to answer prolongs the family's uncertainty, making it difficult to know whether they should keep waiting or begin to mourn. [São Paulo Truth Commission](https://comissaodaverdade.al.sp.gov.br/mortos-desaparecidos/rubens-beirodt-paiva)

Much of her effort goes into keeping fear from taking over the household. Meals, conversations and outings continue, while her composure conceals how much has changed. When she confronts the men watching her home, the anger becomes explicit: “What are you looking at? What do you want? Where’s my husband?” [CNN](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ampr/date/2025-02-14/segment/01)

Her search for answers becomes a demand for the state to acknowledge its actions. Salles follows how the disappearance changes what Eunice can tell her children and what they understand about their father. Each must confront the loss at a different time. Marcelo’s memoir and Salles’s film turn those family memories into a public account of the disappearance. [Salles and Torres](https://letterboxd.com/journal/im-still-here-fernanda-torres-walter-salles-interview/)

### B

In Walter Salles’s *I’m Still Here* (2024), Eunice Paiva must raise five children after Brazil’s military regime takes her husband, Rubens, from their Rio home in 1971. The film adapts their son Marcelo’s memoir. Its concern is how a family continues living when the government responsible for an absence refuses to explain it. [Sony Pictures Classics](https://www.sonyclassics.com/film/imstillhere/)

Before the arrest, their house is a place for music, visitors and discussion. Salles spends time with that everyday freedom, showing what the children expect from home. Afterward, Eunice tries to preserve it while searching for information about Rubens. She and a daughter have also been detained; the children’s protection now depends on someone who has experienced the regime’s intimidation herself. [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/arts/13970569/review-im-still-here-brazils-military-dictatorship)

When journalists come to photograph the family, Eunice insists that they smile, despite the request for a less cheerful picture. She is speaking to the press because she wants Rubens’s disappearance investigated. The smile expresses a refusal to let the family be seen only through its suffering. It does not mean that the loss has been accepted or that the fear has passed. [CNN](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ampr/date/2025-02-14/segment/01)

The household’s memories preserve Rubens as someone they knew, against an official version that evades responsibility for taking him. The state’s refusal to answer therefore affects both the family’s grief and the history that can be publicly acknowledged. [Salles and Torres](https://letterboxd.com/journal/im-still-here-fernanda-torres-walter-salles-interview/)
