## Painting: The Death of Marat — Jacques-Louis David, 1793

A follows the assassination, public commission, and display. B explains how selected details construct a political martyr. Writing block 31584.

### A

Jacques-Louis David painted *The Death of Marat* in 1793, after the assassination of the revolutionary journalist Jean-Paul Marat. Charlotte Corday, a political opponent, stabbed him in his bath. Marat often worked there because bathing relieved a severe skin condition.

David was himself a deputy in the National Convention, the assembly governing France. The Convention commissioned the painting after Marat’s death. His task was to commemorate a political ally as someone who had died in service to the Revolution.

The painting draws on Christian images of sacrifice. Marat’s lowered head and hanging arm recall representations of the dead Christ, particularly Caravaggio’s *The Entombment of Christ*. David uses that familiar visual language to give a contemporary political death the status of martyrdom.

The finished painting hung at the Convention beside David’s portrait of another murdered deputy, Le Peletier. These images placed the Revolution’s dead before the assembly conducting its work.

### B

In Jacques-Louis David’s *The Death of Marat* (1793), a murdered revolutionary appears as a figure worthy of public devotion. The simple wooden box, the writing materials, and the still body present a man interrupted while serving others.

Marat had been a combative journalist who called for the execution of perceived enemies of the Revolution. Charlotte Corday, a political opponent, killed him. David concentrates the viewer’s attention on his dead ally, giving him the dignity associated with a martyr.

The Christian references help explain this choice. The hanging arm recalls images of Christ’s body after the Crucifixion. The plain furnishings suggest modest living, while the pen keeps Marat’s work visible after his death.

David belonged to the revolutionary government that commissioned the painting. His image gave that government a sympathetic way to commemorate a controversial figure. The distinction between Marat’s political record and his painted appearance is part of what makes the work revealing.

## Book: The Picture of Dorian Gray — Oscar Wilde, 1891 edition

A follows the wish into the relationship between appearance and reputation. B uses the Sibyl episode to connect aesthetic pleasure with the treatment of another person. Writing block 42695.

### A

Oscar Wilde’s *The Picture of Dorian Gray* follows a young man who wants to keep his beauty while his portrait grows old. Lord Henry has persuaded him that youth matters above everything else. Looking at the finished painting, Dorian tells its painter, Basil Hallward:

> I am jealous of everything whose beauty does not die.

His wish is fulfilled, but the portrait records more than age. It also changes with his cruelty and selfishness. Dorian can hide it while continuing to meet others with an apparently innocent face. His unchanged appearance helps protect his reputation as he pursues pleasure.

His appearance also affects how others judge him. Basil struggles to believe the rumours about Dorian because he assumes character must be visible:

> Sin is a thing that writes itself across a man’s face.

If people expect cruelty to appear on a face, Dorian’s beauty becomes evidence in his favour. The reader has access to the portrait they cannot see.

### B

Oscar Wilde’s *The Picture of Dorian Gray* follows a young man whose portrait changes with his conduct while he remains beautiful. Lord Henry encourages him to pursue experience and pleasure. The novel explores what happens when Dorian begins judging people by the sensations they provide.

Dorian falls in love with Sibyl Vane, an actress whose performances let him encounter Shakespeare’s heroines. When she acts badly one evening, she explains that loving him has changed her sense of reality:

> You are more to me than all art can ever be.

Dorian sees the loss of what attracted him. He tells her:

> Without your art, you are nothing.

He has valued the experiences her performances gave him more than the person now speaking to him. The first change he then notices in his portrait is cruelty around the mouth. The painting registers his treatment of Sibyl while his own face remains unchanged.

## Film: Parasite — Bong Joon Ho, 2019

A develops unequal dependence and the role of material security. B follows aspiration and the limits of advancement through employment. Writing block 53706.

### A

Bong Joon Ho’s *Parasite* (2019) follows two families in Seoul whose lives become linked through work. The Kims live in a cramped semi-basement. When their son Ki-woo becomes the wealthy Parks’ English tutor, he finds opportunities for his relatives.

The Parks rely on other people to teach their children, drive their car, and run their home. The Kims need the wages, but entering the house does not give them the security its owners enjoy. Their scheme requires deceiving the Parks and displacing other workers. Their gains come partly at the expense of people whose jobs are as insecure as their own.

When Ki-taek praises Mrs Park’s kindness, his wife Chung-sook replies:

> She’s nice because she’s rich.

She connects kindness with the comfort of having one’s needs met. The film examines a class divide that persists even when encounters across it are polite. The Parks decide whom to employ, while the Kims depend on keeping their jobs.

### B

Bong Joon Ho’s *Parasite* (2019) begins with a poor family finding a possible route into a wealthier life. Ki-woo secures an interview to tutor the daughter of the affluent Park family. His sister prepares a false university certificate for him. He explains to his father:

> I just printed out the document early.

He expects to become the person the paper describes. His deception depends on recognizing what the Parks trust: prestigious education, confident manners, and recommendations from people they already know.

The film gives the Kims intelligence and initiative, then examines the limits of what those qualities can achieve. Their successful performance wins jobs inside a wealthy household. They remain employees whose security depends on the family they serve, and their opportunities come through replacing other workers.

The Parks buy educational advantages for their children while Ki-woo invents a university background to obtain work. Through this contrast, the film questions the promise that talent and effort alone can overcome the distance between the families.
