## Editorial purpose and order of importance

Help readers understand a work and learn something worth remembering through it. Explain its central concern or distinctive achievement, the human experience it develops, and relevant social, historical or cultural connections.

Priorities, in order:

1. **Understand the work.** A correct date or interesting anecdote cannot compensate for missing its central meaning.
2. **Explain how it develops that meaning.** Connect specific actions, details, relationships or artistic choices to their consequences.
3. **Choose worthwhile evidence.** Context and quotations should deepen that account or give readers a meaningful encounter with the work.
4. **Write clearly and with restraint.** Readability matters, but it cannot make an incomplete account sufficient.

Do not force every work into a moral lesson. A work may centre ritual, use, formal invention, perception, pleasure or documentation. Explain a supported central reading without pretending it is the only possible meaning.

The separate Grace of Pascal project's saved instructions add a useful emphasis: use the work to understand the subject it depicts. Establish the relevant society, belief, practice, event or relationship before centring the artist's biography. Explain what particular objects, gestures and actions mean in that setting. Then distinguish the work's account from independent evidence about the subject. Artist context belongs where it explains a specific choice. See the [recovered instructions and direct feedback](research-2026-09-24/GRACE_OF_PASCAL_FINDINGS.md).

## 1. Research before deciding what the entry is about

Identify the exact work, creator, version and dates. Read the available Notion entry and relevant selected passages. Treat them as editorial clues, not an independently verified factual database.

Build the interpretation from three kinds of evidence:

- **The work itself:** the relevant image, text, scenes, recording or object documentation. For books, inspect the relevant opening, any author's preface that explains the work's purpose, and passages that develop the main conflict. Distinguish an author's preface from a later editor's introduction. Understand the full arc privately even when the entry withholds its outcome.
- **Substantial interpretation:** read a relevant museum account, critic, journalist, scholar, translator's commentary or creator interview. Search for these for each new work. Follow a second substantial account when the first is narrow, disputed or mostly plot summary. A metadata page, quotation list or promotional synopsis cannot substitute for interpretation.
- **Public reception:** search reasoned reader or viewer discussions, including Reddit and Goodreads, for what people found central or affecting. Look for recurring explanations anchored in specific passages or scenes. Read replies and disagreements. Record visible engagement only when inspected; never call a result popular or representative because search ranked it highly. Resonance is a clue to investigate, not a vote establishing truth or creator intent.

Use Wikipedia as an allowed research and prose reference. Follow citations for important or disputed claims. Museum material, substantial blogs and YouTube criticism may add useful interpretations. Verify video quotations against the video or a reliable transcript; a title is not evidence of its argument.

Consult sources in the subject's or work's original language when feasible, translating relevant findings into English. Especially for an outsider's depiction, research the represented society directly. Do not let an art-movement label or representation critique substitute for its own history and practices. State uncertainty where cultural identification is disputed.

Use the [editorial reference library](REFERENCE_LIBRARY.md) to find independent video essays, podcasts, blogs, critics and public discussions. For each entry, record the specific material actually read or viewed and what it changes in the account. Distinguish full transcripts, accessible essays about videos, partial previews and unexamined leads. A list of well-known channels cannot substitute for consulting their work. Keep museum and primary evidence for factual checks; seek independent criticism to broaden or challenge the interpretation. The September 27 [painting rewrite record](research-2026-09-27/painting-rewrite/README.md) demonstrates the distinction between a factual source and an explanatory model.

Search to challenge the emerging account. Ask what a substantial critic or engaged reader thinks is missing. Do not keep gathering sources that merely confirm the first plausible interpretation. If a useful source is inaccessible, record that limitation and find a substantive replacement. Do not claim to have read it from a search snippet.

Distinguish four things in notes: observed evidence, documented facts, attributed interpretation, and our editorial inference. A critic's account is not automatically the creator's intention. A character's statement is not automatically the author's belief.

## 2. Write a short meaning brief before drafting

Use this private working note, with source links and passage or scene locations:

- **Central account:** What does the work reveal, question or achieve? State the connection, not just labels such as class, trauma or alienation.
- **Human experience:** What do people want, fear, believe or lose? How does the work make that experience understandable?
- **Development:** Which concrete details or changes establish the account? Explain why they matter together.
- **World connection:** Which social practice, history, belief or cultural setting makes this account clearer? Do not invent a connection where none is useful.
- **Subject context:** What will a reader learn about the represented society or human experience? Which specific details justify that account? Do not duplicate the world connection if one note answers both.
- **Challenge and omission:** Which substantial alternative or contradiction did research reveal? What would make this account misleading if left out?

List substantial concerns before narrowing. Then identify the evidence the finished entry needs. Do not label every theme covered merely because it appears somewhere in the notes.

Examples of past failures:

- *The Ambassadors*: a technical mention of anamorphosis was insufficient without the skull, mortality and its Christian cultural context.
- *The Raft of the Medusa*: rescue and political abandonment were incomplete without the violence and cannibalism among those stranded. Explain the connection without claiming the canvas visibly depicts cannibalism.
- *Gatsby*: aspiration and class exclusion were insufficient without moral conduct, unequal consequences and Nick's mixed judgment.
- *All Quiet*: patriotic education, survival and home leave became an inadequate account when bodily violence and destroyed youth were barely developed. The preface and opening already offered stronger guidance.

These examples define omission failures. They are not a compulsory theme list for other works.

## 3. Select passages before polishing

When passages or dialogue would enrich the entry, compare a small shortlist. Consider what each contributes to the whole entry, not merely the paragraph beside it.

A selection can offer a consequential moment, distinctive writing, an interesting perspective, or insight into creation and reception. Adapt the choice to genre. Humor, sensory detail and practical information can matter without carrying a moral lesson.

Round 16's Animal Farm A shows that a complete central account can still benefit from a short character passage. Julio requested a Boxer paragraph and quotation, with little setup. Add such passages without rewriting preferred prose or requiring each quotation to carry the whole interpretation.

For each candidate, record privately:

- What the exact words or scene help the reader understand.
- Whether this is central evidence, a useful complement, or an attractive distraction.
- The context needed, including speaker and any change in meaning across the exchange.
- Why this candidate contributes more than the alternatives.

Familiarity is neither a reason to choose nor reject a passage. Compare defining openings, prefaces and important exchanges with less famous choices. Do not select the shortest verified sentence simply because it fits easily. When brevity makes a quotation trivial, use another selection or paraphrase the larger passage accurately.

*Good Will Hunting* round 15 illustrates the distinction: Sean's memory of his wife's habit was relevant to affection within that scene. Its exact quoted sentence did too little for the entry's account of Will. A locally relevant quotation can still be the wrong choice.

The context should remain understandable without the quotation. Removing a well-chosen passage should still lose useful evidence, insight or a direct encounter with the work. Do not pre-explain it so completely that it becomes decorative confirmation. Multiple passages should have distinct functions; no universal number or length has been approved.

Verify the wording and surrounding passage. Preserve the source's voice; our plain-language rules do not authorize simplifying literary quotations. Keep translations and paraphrases clearly distinguished. For film, use the released version or a reliable transcription with clear provenance. A draft screenplay may differ from the film. Never claim direct audio verification after checking only a transcript.

Keep source, contributor, edition, translator, language and locator in the separate source record. A Kindle location is not a page. Do not append redundant credit lines such as “all excerpts are from…” to the reading text. Natural speaker and scene context still belongs there. Respect the applicable quotation limits without letting a tiny quote replace meaningful analysis.

## 4. Write the connected explanation

Orient the reader to the subject or premise. Then develop the central account through concrete evidence. Chronology often helps paintings about historical events. A book or film may need a causal or emotional sequence instead. Do not impose an identical paragraph structure on every medium.

Explain why the detail matters. A succession of things that happen is still plot summary. A theme named in the last sentence cannot repair an entry organized around peripheral material.

Check whether the finished entry carries the historical facts needed to understand its central account. A source ledger can contain the right evidence while the prose still omits it. In historical film or nonfiction, distinguish what participants knew then from what the historical record establishes now. Spoiler restraint must not turn a documented killing into an unresolved disappearance. Select political, economic or biographical context for its explanatory value; do not append a fact list as a repair.

Assume the reader has never encountered the work or remembers little of it. Introduce the people, their relationship and the practical consequence before naming the wider social structure. For example, explain that protesting underpayment risks both a job and a home before discussing dependence on landowners. Historical context still matters; unexplained political changes or economic terms must not replace a clear causal account. Julio endorsed the [Vidas Secas paragraph replacement](CALIBRATION_18_PARAGRAPH_REVISION.md) as “great. much better”; retain it as a concrete example of this sequence.

Write for a high school reader with little prior knowledge of the subject. A famous name or religious title does not supply its own context. Explain who Agrippa and Augustus were, what changed under Augustus, or why a Buddha matters to worshippers when the entry depends on that knowledge. Keep those explanations close to the detail they clarify.

Select technical details for what they explain. In the Pantheon, lighter and thinner concrete is useful because it reduces the weight the dome must support. Dimensions and the sphere that fits inside do less for this account. Do not add measurements or terminology merely to sound informed. Explain the practical reason, then connect it to the building's use or history where useful.

Use social and historical context to explain a practice, relationship or artistic choice. An informative, mildly opinionated argument is welcome when its basis is clear. Explain how scientific lectures operated as a business, for example, before turning a painted experiment into a moral judgment. Verify historical anecdotes; a conversational example from Julio is not automatically a fact.

Keep necessary cruelty, conflict and emotion. Restraint applies to our rhetoric, not to the seriousness of the work. Describe atrocities plainly when they are central. Do not substitute “hardship” or “uncertainty” for the actual experience merely to keep the tone calm.

Use familiar words, concrete nouns and active verbs. **Vary sentence length and keep related ideas connected.** Julio's September 26 correction rejects the run of short, separate sentences that developed from the earlier under-twenty-word preference. A longer sentence is useful when it clearly explains a cause, contrast or consequence; a short sentence can then give the reader a pause. Use connectors where they express the actual relationship, rather than mechanically adding transitions. Do not replace plain language with a rigid sentence-length rule. Avoid jargon, elevated metaphors, decorative contrasts, praise, instructions about what readers must feel, and generic conclusions about humanity. Avoid a dramatic final verdict. End with the last useful explanation.

Read each paragraph as an argument or story, not a sequence of captions. Ask what the next sentence adds and why it follows. Prefer an explained social practice, a specific historical development or a revealing artistic decision to an inventory of objects or a broad thematic label. Do not force an additional paragraph simply to restate the central interpretation. Keep the plain vocabulary and chronological clarity of selected A/B examples while allowing the prose to breathe.

Apply that clarity to specialist media too. Explain an audible change before relying on musical terminology; explain a character's relationships and practical stakes before naming a dramatic conflict. A source-supported sentence can still confuse a beginner. The later Beethoven and Eternal Sunshine notes demonstrate this failure. Pascal's earlier four-paragraph museum-label sequence is an available reference, not a fixed template or word limit for Taste.

Be succinct without imposing an arbitrary word ceiling. No universal entry length has been approved. Cut repetition, scene setup and peripheral facts before compressing the explanation of meaning.

## Literature: a short foreword

Julio named the model on 26 September: a foreword by a knowledgeable journalist or specialist, made easy to read. Use **four to five short paragraphs maximum** for a literature entry. A standalone excerpt occupies one of those blocks. This is a literature-specific limit, not a universal formula for all media.

Introduce enough of the story to orient a reader who has never read it. Explain the author’s central concern through the characters, choices, relationships or narrative voice that develop it. Add author and publication context when it explains the book’s significance. Keep each transition between fiction, the narrator and real history clear. Use simple language without flattening the book’s ideas into a slogan. This should be an informed invitation to understand the work, not a plot recap, promotional blurb or dramatic verdict.

Choose passages that offer a meaningful encounter with the writing. Keep edition and translation credits in the source record. Avoid revealing late outcomes when an earlier detail carries the same insight. Leave out praise and technical vocabulary that do not help a first-time reader.

## Painting feedback: context before specialist language

Explain the people and the story behind a scene. Saturn needs the relationship between Roman Saturn and Greek Cronus, the prophecy, and the family conflict. The Black Paintings need their private domestic setting and relevant, documented circumstances of Goya’s life. Do not turn illness or disturbing imagery into an unsupported psychiatric diagnosis.

Seek substantial museum videos and explanatory art criticism when a draft lacks an interesting, supported account. Inspect the video or transcript before attributing an argument to it. If a transcript is unavailable, record that limit and verify the proposed details through the museum’s written account. A source’s popularity cannot substitute for accuracy.

When feedback rejects a dramatic closing sentence, remove it. Add useful history only when requested or needed. Preserve surrounding prose that was not criticized. The September 26 Nighthawks and Gulf Stream notes require deletions; Lady Jane Grey also requires the sequence from succession dispute to rebellion and execution.

## 5. Keep the frames clear

- Establish fiction as fiction. Separate the story's setting from publication or release dates.
- Signal moves from events inside the story to real history, creation, reception or the author's commentary. One clear cue can govern a paragraph.
- Distinguish narrator, character, creator, critic and our interpretation. Attribute nonfiction arguments rather than presenting them automatically as established findings.
- Separate an artwork's representation from the actual event or community. Verify broader cultural claims independently. Desk research is not original ethnographic fieldwork.
- Do not infer private feelings, diagnoses or motives from a painted gesture. Do not generalize a fictional household or social critique to an entire population.

Do not add generic reminders that an artwork is interpretive or is not a literal record of reality. Julio rejected this caveat in Stańczyk. Keep necessary factual distinctions, but include them only when a specific misunderstanding would change the account. Do not end a paragraph with a disclaimer that teaches the reader nothing about the work.

Include premise and thematic plot details needed to understand the work. Normally omit late reversals and outcomes. Understand them during research; withholding them from the reader must not erase the central emotional or ethical account. Explain the concern without naming an outcome where possible. If a requested analysis truly needs the ending, signal the spoiler boundary.

## 6. Review the finished prose against the evidence

Review interpretation separately from factual accuracy. Do this before presenting an entry:

1. **Read the draft alone.** State what a new reader would understand from it. Compare that with the source-supported meaning brief. A gap requires revision, not a claim that the theme was implied.
2. **Trace the explanation.** Identify where the actual prose connects evidence to meaning. If the crucial connection exists only in research notes, add it or change the structure.
3. **Challenge the selection.** Form a brief account directly from substantive criticism and work evidence before looking back at the drafting brief. Compare the accounts, using public reception to locate overlooked concerns. Identify the strongest omitted evidence and explain why its omission does not distort the work.
4. **Check each quotation in the whole entry.** Reject a passage that redirects attention to a secondary point or merely restates nearby prose.
5. **Verify facts and boundaries.** Check names, dates, event identification, source/version, exact quotations, interpretation versus intent, fiction versus history, and necessary context.
6. **Edit against a relevant successful reference.** Preserve clarity and depth together. Remove vague theme labels, descriptive inventory, strained argument and ornamental endings.

Do not present a draft while a central omission or unsupported interpretation remains unresolved. Research and revise internally. Where sources genuinely disagree, retain the disagreement or qualify the reading. Do not ask Julio to supply a basic interpretation that available sources could resolve.

A checklist, source count, word count, or agreement between models does not establish editorial success. Reviews must identify the source evidence and the corresponding prose, including weaknesses. When a separate reviewer is used, have them form their account from the sources before seeing the drafter's interpretation. They should challenge the account rather than inherit its conclusion. Another agent is not required for every entry; the lead writer remains responsible for final choices.

When A/B comparisons are requested, both versions must independently explain the essential account. Vary sequence, emphasis or supporting evidence. Do not offer a knowingly incomplete B or duplicate a flawed interpretation twice. These are qualitative comparisons, not statistical validation. Otherwise, normally deliver one finished entry.

## Adapt without forcing a template

| Form | Evidence that may matter |
|---|---|
| Painting, drawing, print | Represented event, production, meaningful visual choices, historical context and reception. Select details that explain the work. |
| Books and poetry | Broad concern, narrative voice, emotional or intellectual development, textual evidence and relevant context. Distinguish author, narrator and translator. |
| Film | Central conflict, emotional development, scene evidence, performance and form where useful. Production facts alone are insufficient. |
| Photography | Exact image or series, caption, access, staging, purpose and circulation. Do not replace the image with a career biography. |
| Objects, sculpture, craft, architecture | Making, material, use, location, patronage and changes in function. |
| Music | Exact composition or recording, performers, arrangement, audible evidence and relevant performance history. |
| Performance and cultural practices | Particular version or occasion, participants, purpose, transmission and community sources. Avoid inventing a timeless universal form. |

Photography is now an eighth category in the editorial review. This does not import content or change the main app's catalog. Current calibration is concentrated in paintings, books and films. It does not establish success across all forms.
