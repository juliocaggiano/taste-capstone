## Complete A with only that paragraph changed

Published in 1938, Graciliano Ramos’s *Vidas Secas* follows a fictional family of *retirantes*: people driven from their homes by drought and poverty. Fabiano, Sinha Vitória, their two boys and their dog, Baleia, cross Brazil’s northeastern interior looking for food and work. Finding a farm where they can stay offers relief, but little security.

Fabiano works as a cattle herder on land he does not own. His employer pays him less than he is owed. When Fabiano complains, the man tells him to look for work elsewhere. Losing the job would also mean losing the family’s home. This dependence helps explain why their poverty continues even when the drought ends. [João Roberto Maia](https://www.scielo.br/j/alea/a/rNBYPktCKPyMkZTp875f7Gj/)

Ramos had himself been imprisoned without formal charges under Getúlio Vargas in 1936. He later credited living alongside poor prisoners with helping him write about their lives. His narration gives Fabiano thoughts he cannot easily express to an employer or policeman. “You’re an animal, Fabiano,” he tells himself, accepting his low position while also taking pride in his endurance. Sinha Vitória’s calculations, the boys’ curiosity and Baleia’s affection give each a life beyond the work of surviving. [Casa Museu](https://www.casagracilianoramos.com.br/sala-6-memorias-do-carcere/1936-), [Zenir Campos Reis](https://www.scielo.br/j/ea/a/4P76p7GKXFft33XjbtKdLqk/?format=html&lang=pt)

The repeated departures connect their story to Brazil’s history of internal migration. The parents imagine a city where the boys could attend school. The narrator anticipates them becoming trapped there. Elsewhere in Brazil, this movement was already being organised as a source of labor. From 1935, São Paulo subsidised workers’ journeys to its coffee and cotton farms. Leaving the drought could mean entering another economy that needed their labor without guaranteeing them a better life.
