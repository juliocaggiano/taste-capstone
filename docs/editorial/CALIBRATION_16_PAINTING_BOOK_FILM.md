## Stańczyk — Jan Matejko, 1862

### A

In Jan Matejko’s *Stańczyk* (1862), a Polish court jester sits alone while a royal ball continues nearby. The painting’s full title links his distress to the loss of Smolensk, a strategically important fortress captured by Muscovite forces in 1514. Matejko uses this historical defeat to consider how those governing a country respond to its troubles. [National Museum in Warsaw](https://cyfrowe.mnw.art.pl/pl/zbiory/446753)

The man employed to amuse the court appears more concerned about the country than the people attending the ball. His isolation gives the scene its political criticism: serious judgment belongs to the jester, while the court remains occupied with pleasure. The distant festivities make his concern seem unshared.

Matejko painted this scene centuries later, when Poland had lost its independence through partition. For him, national history offered a way to examine political failures and the responsibilities of those in power. He gave Stańczyk his own facial features, identifying himself with the figure who takes those responsibilities seriously. The choice also suggests how he understood his work as a painter: interpreting the country’s past for people living with its consequences. [Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/stanczyk-jan-matejko)

### B

Jan Matejko painted *Stańczyk* in 1862, when Poland was divided under foreign rule. His historical paintings examined the country’s past from that position. In this one, he made a court jester the person most troubled by a national setback. [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/about-us/press-and-media/press-releases-archive/conversations-with-god-jan-matejko-s-copernicus)

The scene takes place at a Renaissance royal ball. Its full title associates the jester’s distress with the loss of Smolensk, a fortress captured by Muscovite forces in 1514. Stańczyk sits apart from the celebration, apparently preoccupied with consequences that appear not to disturb the festivities. Matejko constructs a contrast between the court’s enjoyment and one man’s political awareness. [National Museum in Warsaw](https://cyfrowe.mnw.art.pl/pl/zbiory/446753)

Choosing a jester makes that contrast pointed. Someone whose role is to entertain exercises the judgment missing from the festive court. Matejko also gave him his own features. This connects the Renaissance figure with the nineteenth-century artist, whose paintings asked Polish audiences to consider their history. Stańczyk’s solitude therefore carries a public concern: anxiety about the country’s future in a setting where that concern receives little attention. [Culture.pl](https://culture.pl/en/work/stanczyk-jan-matejko)

## Animal Farm — George Orwell, 1945

### A

In George Orwell’s fable *Animal Farm* (1945), farm animals overthrow an owner who takes the produce of their labour. They hope to share the farm’s resources and live without hunger or the whip. Equality becomes the basis of their new society.

The pigs soon reserve the milk and apples for themselves. Their spokesman, Squealer, claims that their intellectual work requires better food. He presents this privilege as necessary for everyone’s welfare, then warns that the old farmer could return. Fear of losing their freedom helps the pigs secure advantages over the others. [John Molyneux](https://www.marxists.org/history/etol/writers/molyneux/1989/xx/orwell.html)

Napoleon, their leader, abolishes public debate and uses dogs to intimidate and kill other animals. The pigs also alter the rules whenever their conduct conflicts with them. A later version of the farm’s founding principle reads:

> All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.

The rulers preserve the language of equality while giving themselves rights they deny everyone else. [Animal Farm](https://gutenberg.net.au/ebooks01/0100011h.html)

Orwell wrote the fable as a criticism of Stalin’s Soviet Union. He supported socialism, but argued that the Soviet state had established another class hierarchy. The animals’ hopes for a fairer society make the betrayal understandable: their leaders continue to invoke those hopes while exploiting the workers who trusted them. [Orwell Foundation](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/books-by-orwell/animal-farm/preface-to-the-ukrainian-edition-of-animal-farm-by-george-orwell/)

### B

In George Orwell’s fable *Animal Farm* (1945), animals overthrow their owner so they can benefit from their own labour. Boxer, a powerful cart-horse, gives his strength to the shared project. He believes that working harder will help everyone enjoy the freedom they have won.

His answer to difficulties is “I will work harder!” Later, he adopts another rule: “Napoleon is always right.” His commitment to the other animals becomes tied to trust in their leader. When things go wrong, he demands more from himself rather than holding Napoleon responsible. The pigs benefit from his willingness to keep sacrificing for a cause whose rewards they increasingly reserve for themselves. [John Molyneux](https://www.marxists.org/history/etol/writers/molyneux/1989/xx/orwell.html)

Other animals have reasons to doubt the leadership, but challenging it becomes dangerous. Napoleon ends public debates, and his dogs enforce his authority through threats and killings. The pigs rewrite rules and explain away their privileges. Work continues in the name of a common good while the workers lose their say in how the farm is run. [Animal Farm](https://gutenberg.net.au/ebooks01/0100011h.html)

The historical target was Stalin’s Soviet Union. Orwell wanted readers to distinguish its claims of socialism from the unequal society it had produced. Boxer helps explain the human cost of that deception: his loyalty and effort sustain rulers who give him little control over the life he is working to improve. [Orwell Foundation](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/books-by-orwell/animal-farm/preface-to-the-ukrainian-edition-of-animal-farm-by-george-orwell/)

## Soul — Pete Docter and Kemp Powers, 2020

### A

In *Soul* (2020), New York music teacher Joe Gardner believes his real life will begin when he becomes a professional jazz pianist. He loves playing, but years of unsuccessful auditions have made everything else feel secondary. His fear comes out in a conversation with his mother:

> I’m just afraid that if I died today, that my life would have amounted to nothing. [Fresh Air](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/soul-creators-passion-purpose-and-realizing-youre-enough-0)

An accident interrupts the day he finally lands a coveted gig. In the film’s imaginary world before birth, he meets 22, a soul who has never found a reason to want life on Earth. Repeated failures to discover a calling have left her feeling inadequate. Joe becomes her mentor, believing that finding a passion will solve the problem. He also needs her help to return to his performance.

Their journey back goes wrong, leaving 22 temporarily in Joe’s body. She enjoys experiences he takes for granted: tasting pizza, watching a seed drift from a tree, talking with people. These encounters make her want more time on Earth before she has any ambition for what to do there. [TIME](https://time.com/5923340/soul-review-pixar/)

The film questions the pressure to make a career justify an entire life. Joe’s love of music is real, but treating it as his only reason to exist makes everything else seem like wasted time. It also leads him to dismiss the experiences through which 22 is beginning to want a life of her own. [Sight and Sound](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/soul-pixar-disney-pete-docter-kemp-powers-jamie-foxx-jazz-musician-fantasy)

### B

In Pixar’s *Soul* (2020), Joe Gardner is a New York music teacher certain that jazz is his reason for existing. “I was born to play,” he tells his students. An accident carries him into an imaginary realm before birth, where he meets 22, a soul who has never wanted to live. Joe fears missing the career he was meant to have; 22 fears that having no calling makes her unfit for life. [Fresh Air](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/soul-creators-passion-purpose-and-realizing-youre-enough-0)

They return to New York together, with 22 accidentally inhabiting Joe’s body. At his barbershop, she asks questions he has never thought to ask. His barber, Dez, once wanted to become a veterinarian. Circumstances led him into cutting hair, and he finds satisfaction in his work and the people he meets. Joe has known him for years without learning this, because their conversations usually revolve around jazz. [Visual Parables](https://readthespirit.com/visual-parables/soul-2020/)

Dez’s experience challenges Joe’s assumption that an unfulfilled ambition makes a life unsuccessful. The conversation also reveals how little Joe knows about someone he sees regularly. His focus on a future performance has narrowed his interest in the life already around him.

22 begins to enjoy that life through food, conversation and watching the city. She has yet to find a profession she wants to pursue, but she is becoming curious about living. Music remains a source of pleasure for Joe; their time together questions his belief that everyone needs an equivalent calling.
