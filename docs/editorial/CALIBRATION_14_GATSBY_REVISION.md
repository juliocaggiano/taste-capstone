## Diagnosis after source review — editorial inference

The Gatsby drafts foregrounded recovering Daisy and undoing the past. They made class primarily an obstacle to romance and criminal wealth a background fact. The prior source audit recognized moral contradiction and unequal consequences but excluded their explanation to avoid ending spoilers. That was an editorial choice, not a lack of evidence.

Nick's admiration for Gatsby's hope coexists with his disapproval. Gatsby is not an innocent moral alternative to the established rich; their inherited position does not establish moral worth. ## Revised entry

F. Scott Fitzgerald’s *The Great Gatsby* (1925) follows a society in which wealth brings admiration and protects people from consequences. Its narrator, Nick Carraway, enters this world during the novel’s summer of 1922.

His neighbour Gatsby has risen from poverty and wants to recover his former relationship with Daisy Buchanan. She represents both someone he loves and the wealthy life he has imagined for himself. His fortune, made through criminal dealings, pays for the mansion and parties intended to attract her. Yet Daisy’s husband, Tom, still regards him as an inferior. Acquiring money has not erased the distinction between inherited status and newly purchased possessions.

Tom’s own position requires no comparable effort. He is unfaithful to Daisy and violent toward his mistress, while continuing to claim social superiority. Through him, the novel separates the privileges of wealth from any evidence of deserving them. Nick describes Tom and Daisy as people who:

> smashed up things and creatures and then retreated back into their money

That protection lets them leave others to bear the consequences of their choices.

Nick nevertheless admires Gatsby’s capacity to hope, even while disapproving of his conduct. The novel’s criticism of the American dream includes this contradiction: Gatsby’s desire for a better life becomes tied to gaining acceptance among people whose behaviour gives him little reason to admire them.
