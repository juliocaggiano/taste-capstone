# Batch 002 — architecture research and image check

Prepared 25 September 2026. Five new review drafts. None approved or released. Read against VOICE_GUIDE v0.20 and IMAGE_REVIEW_WORKFLOW.md. Individual source URLs and precise claim support are in `architecture.json`; each image’s original record, download URL, dimensions and SHA-256 are in `architecture-images.json`.

## Editorial approach

Each draft establishes who needed the building, explains a relevant spatial or construction choice, and connects that choice to social or religious life. Technical details remain only when their practical consequence is understandable. No measurements, invented symbolism, decorative dramatic ending, or generic caveat that architecture is interpretive. Architecture needs an evidenced historical account rather than an invented single authorial message.

Primary institutional interpretation was checked against the actual plans, sections and engravings. Public discussion was used to spot likely omissions and misconceptions, never as authority. Some Reddit and Smarthistory pages returned errors on direct opening; only retrieved indexed passages informed those checks, as recorded in the entry metadata. No claim to having read inaccessible full pages.

## Hagia Sophia

**Central account.** Justinian replaced the church destroyed during revolt. The open domed space links difficult construction with a Christian imperial setting. Its later conversions explain why multiple communities care about it.

**Read evidence.** Emma Wegner’s Met essay establishes the 532 destruction, architects, 537 inauguration, load-bearing arrangement, contemporary association with heaven, 558 dome failure, 562 rebuilding and Ottoman conversion. An indexed Smarthistory account provides the more specific relationship between concentrated supports and windows. UNESCO’s 2020 statement and mission report establish the recent change of use. The old Met essay’s museum description is not treated as current.

**Challenge.** A broad popular history encountered during research gives inconsistent collapse dates and a false largest-dome comparison. Those claims were excluded. The original dome did not survive intact. The draft does not make all Byzantine churches resemble this exceptional commission.

**Images.** A: Salzenberg’s 1854 architectural study. B: an explicitly reconstructed church section published by Lübke and Semrau in 1908. C: Grelot’s engraved plan from his 1680 book. All are historical studies, not original sixth-century working drawings. B’s historic statue and furnishings are reconstructed details; they are not claims about the present building. C retains original paper folds and damage. Its exact scan-reuse terms remain unverified, clearly separated from the public-domain historical print.

## The Alhambra

**Central account.** A fortified Nasrid court city combines everyday palace organization with statements of royal and religious authority. The fountain ties a small visible detail to hydraulic control and court poetry.

**Read evidence.** The Patronato’s history explains the palace city and Nasrid phases. Its Court of the Lions interpretation explains Muhammad V’s commission and rooms with multiple uses. The Comares poetry essay identifies religious texts and praise of rulers. The conservation account explains the fountain’s concealed pipes, gentle flow and Ibn Zamrak’s poem.

**Challenge.** No claim that the palace exclusively housed a harem, that its original courtyard planting is settled, or that the lions were a mysterious water clock. Public comments prompted checking restoration history: later high jets and added basin configurations differ from the original water effect. The draft paraphrases the basin poem without importing its rhetorical style.

**Images.** All three downloaded directly from Real Academia de Bellas Artes de San Fernando object records: Juan de Villanueva’s Court of the Lions (MA-0540, 1766), Comares section (MA-0541, 1766–1767), and Francisco Antonio Contreras Muñoz’s Hall of the Kings (MA-0204, c. 1856). These are later documentary drawings prepared for printed architectural publications. B’s right edge is the existing source boundary, not a local crop. C includes the full tall sheet. Underlying drawings are historical public-domain works; the museum’s scan credit and public-distribution clearance remain explicit.

## Villa Rotonda

**Central account.** Paolo Almerico commissioned an elite residence following his career at the papal court. Four temple fronts confer status on a home and engage the landscape. The central room makes the organization legible; publication explains the design’s wider availability.

**Read evidence.** The Palladio Museum / CISA specialist entry was used for the commission, construction period, non-agricultural character, classification among palaces, geometry and completion. The Met’s commentary on the 1570 woodcut corroborates domestic retreat, temple porches, central dome, countryside and later imitation.

**Challenge.** This is not explained as a generic productive Palladian farm estate. The dome’s final authorship and extent of Scamozzi’s changes are not overspecified. Looking through the house is an explanatory reading of the published plan, not a claimed quotation from Palladio.

**Images.** A/B: later engraved section and plan from Bertotti Scamozzi’s 1776–1783 publication, digitized by the National Library of Poland. The section is signed David Rossi. B is an existing Commons extraction credited to Redaktor GLAM, whose crop, desaturation and contrast adjustment are disclosed. C: Palladio’s 1570 book illustration from the Met, with plan and combined elevation/section. C is the published design while construction continued, not an exact survey of the completed building.

## The Crystal Palace

**Central account.** Industrial fabrication became an architectural method for a temporary exhibition hall. Its international display also organized nations and colonies around Britain’s trade and imperial position. The museum connection provides a concrete legacy.

**Read evidence.** Julius Bryant’s substantial V&A account supplies Paxton’s greenhouse experience, standardized parts, timber as well as iron and glass, protected trees, distribution of display space, and the later Sydenham building. Berlyn and Fowler’s 1851 contemporary book was consulted in full searchable transcription, especially its design and general-description sections and illustration list. The V&A museum-building history explains educational purpose and the subsequent collection.

**Challenge.** All three images show the original Hyde Park building. The enlarged Sydenham structure opened in 1854; its 1936 fire is not assigned to a building still standing in Hyde Park. The rejected committee proposal on page 22 of the source book was excluded. No unsupported claim of the first prefabricated building or first use of plate glass. The contemporary authors’ enthusiastic imperial language is not adopted as the draft’s voice.

**Images.** All three are published in the 1851 book: transverse section/frontispiece; ground plan/page 34; portion of principal elevations/page 37. A is a section with receding interior perspective, C is a façade detail with slight drawn perspective. Neither is falsely labeled a strictly orthographic modern measured elevation. Their original broad proportions are preserved. B is a full plan, not an alternate building.

## St Peter’s Basilica

**Central account.** The venerated burial site of Peter explains pilgrimage and papal authority. Successive architects maintained that center. Construction financing connects the building to the indulgence controversy without turning it into the sole cause of the Reformation.

**Read evidence.** Pietro Zander’s accounts for the Fabric of Saint Peter establish the tomb, successive churches and altars, pilgrimage and rebuilding. The official chronology establishes 1506–1626. Dewey Weiss Kramer’s substantial introduction to Tetzel’s rebuttal, published by Emory’s Pitts Theology Library, establishes the 1515 St Peter’s indulgence and Luther’s 1517 challenge. The Met’s Reformation essay supplies broader context. Catholic treatment of Peter as first pope and later popes as successors is attributed as belief, with Vatican News support.

**Challenge.** Indulgences are not relics, and official Catholic doctrine did not simply sell forgiveness for unrepented future sins. The draft distinguishes punishment remaining after forgiveness from the concern about promotional practices. Neither the tomb identification nor papal succession is presented as an uncontested secular historical finding.

**Images.** Dupérac’s exterior, section and plan after Michelangelo are three distinct historical prints from the Met. Each is explicitly a proposed design before Maderno’s extension, not the present building. The Met title and date fields differ for A/B: titles identify publication in 1569, while date fields record 1558–61. This discrepancy is preserved in metadata, not silently resolved. All three images are CC0 Open Access.

## Word-limit and source-use check

Each body is original synthesis, with no direct quotation. No source contributes more than its retrieved 200-word allowance. Hagia Sophia distributes its account across the Met, structural interpretation and UNESCO. Rotonda splits specialist commission history from the Met’s spatial/print account. Crystal Palace separates the main V&A account from the historical book and museum-building history. Alhambra and St Peter’s use distinct institutional or scholarly accounts for different parts of the argument. Source titles and metadata identify provenance without repeating attributions inside the reading text.

## Image verification and limitations

- 15 JPEG assets exist locally, three per entry, directly under `batch-001/image-options/assets/architecture/b002-*`.
- Every image was individually opened and visually inspected. Subjects, labeled drawing type, full sheet or existing source crop, legibility and source condition were checked.
- Dimensions were measured locally. JPEG headers and SHA-256 values were verified against the manifest.
- No local cropping, recoloring, sharpening, background substitution or image generation was performed.
- Wikimedia originals initially returned rate limits. Seven files use the service’s regular 1280px preview endpoint, following its response guidance. Full-resolution source URLs remain recorded. Museum originals were used for the remaining direct institutional downloads. No request rotation or access-control bypass was used.
- The 1280px versions support the three-column review. Their smaller details have less enlargement latitude than the museum originals. Crystal Palace A/B remain unusually wide because the originals are wide drawings, not because the subject was cropped.
- Scan-license verification and publication permission remain separate from editorial image preference. The Grelot scan and RABASF scans require particular attention before public distribution. Review eligibility is not publication clearance.
- All entries remain pending review, with no writing approval and no chosen image. The existing approved-submissions archive and app catalog were not changed.
