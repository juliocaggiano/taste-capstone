## Transferable lessons across all sixteen

| Saved study | Useful writing direction | Boundary when drafting |
|---|---|---|
| Joey Lawrence | Name people and locate the encounter. A person’s statement, work or circumstances can explain what a photograph opens onto. | His documentary portraits, advertising and celebrity commissions require distinct context. The named caption is evidence; an expressive face alone is not. |
| Sebastião Salgado | Explain people’s material conditions without reducing them to pity or heroic symbols. | A photograph belongs to a series. Do not pretend one frame supplies all the series’ evidence. Verify the collected dates independently. |
| Joel Sternfeld | Use an ordinary detail to complicate a broad social reading. The finance interns’ hot dogs temper their business-world aspirations. | Keep the humor and ambiguity. Do not infer confidence or arrogance as private facts. |
| David Seymour | Children’s situations become understandable through the school, meal, shelter or institution around them. | Historical assignment and original caption matter more than a catalogue of awards. |
| Lynsey Addario | Explain who could enter a place, what access revealed, and what practical risks people faced. | The embedded Phillip Knightley essay is a separate author’s argument. Verify the Bibi Aisha caption attribution before reusing it; being present in this profile is not proof of authorship. |
| Éric Lafforgue | Access restrictions and the photographer’s position can explain an image’s limits. | Reject the page’s inflated praise, sweeping national psychology, unsupported clandestine claims, and misplaced marine-iguana caption. |
| Nicéphore Niépce | Technical detail earns space when it explains what became possible or why an image looks as it does. | Distinguish the surviving plate from later enhanced reproductions. Creation dates require collection research. |
| Josef Koudelka | Connect an exact scene with the event, risk of publication and conditions of circulation. | An invasion photograph and a later exile landscape cannot share a generic explanation just because one artist made both. |
| Joel Meyerowitz | Color, edges, timing and overlapping public activities can explain how a photograph organizes everyday life. | A career-level account must become an image-specific account before publication. |
| Mary Ellen Mark | Give named people relationships and circumstances, retaining ordinary social life alongside hardship. | Do not manufacture biographies from a brief caption or turn people outside the mainstream into a single social type. |
| Felice Beato | Explain how access, military power, commercial audiences and hand-coloring affect the historical record. | War documentation and posed export-market scenes make different claims. Recheck period captions and avoid inheriting derogatory labels as our terms. |
| Harry Gruyaert | Treat color, light, pleasure and composition as meaningful. Place and time need not become a moral argument. | Do not force an ethnographic thesis onto a photograph whose chief achievement is perceptual. |
| Gregory Crewdson | Explain deliberate staging when the invented scene is central to its meaning. | The scene is not evidence that the represented event happened. No diagnosis based on a sitter’s appearance. |
| Ole Jørgen Liodden | Specific weather, place, animal behavior and the circumstances of exposure can reward close attention. | The first-person Arctic story is the photographer’s, not Julio’s. Avoid turning a visible pose into an animal’s intention. |
| Nilüfer Demir | Consider publication, selection and the setting in which audiences saw a photograph. | Distinguish making an image, editors publishing it and later public reactions. Neither fame nor one sympathetic response proves policy effects. |
| Tyler Shields | Identify a staged image’s constructed action and intended viewing context. | Promotional praise and a collaborator’s first-person account are not independent criticism or Julio’s voice. |

## Guidance for the new category

Start with one exact photograph and the subject it makes available for discussion. Establish the people, location and moment. Explain a visible detail through situated evidence. Use nearby photographs in the series to check context, not as interchangeable illustrations of the named work.

For a cultural practice, look for practitioners’ accounts and field research. Preserve disagreements within a community. Ask what the people pictured do with the practice: work, worship, family obligations, education, exchange, enjoyment or self-presentation. The useful question is how it operates in people’s lives, not merely why it looks unfamiliar to visitors.

Keep four layers distinct in research: what is visible; what the original caption establishes; what outside research explains; and our supported interpretation. A photographic expression does not prove a feeling. A practice associated with a community does not establish this sitter’s motive.

Describe the photographic encounter when it changes the account: posed or candid, commissioned or independent, translated or directly spoken, made for a newspaper or for advertising. Do not append boilerplate about every photograph being subjective. Identify the actual choice and consequence.

Build the prose through concrete relationships. End on a useful fact, recorded voice or unresolved disagreement. Avoid an invented moral, sentimental tribute, exotic vocabulary, or a generic appeal to universal humanity.

## New Engalo candidate: what source checking changed

The photographer’s caption is useful for her identity and the exact frame. It is insufficient for a cultural explanation. Specialist sources challenge the caption’s suggested slave-raider origin. Field research supplies women’s own divergent views, including an insistence that cultural appearance and schooling can coexist. These discoveries shape the story; the celebrity biography does not.

One authentic original image was found. Julio’s current instruction permits that single original and rejects artificial variants. Preserve the whole photograph and its published colors. Additional versions must be genuine reproductions of the same exposure, not other people or newly generated images presented as equivalent choices.
