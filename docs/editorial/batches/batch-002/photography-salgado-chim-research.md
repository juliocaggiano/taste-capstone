## Selected works and exact images

- **Cast of Thousands, Serra Pelada**, Sebastião Salgado, 1986. [Danziger Gallery](https://www.danzigergallery.com/artists/sebastiao-salgado) gives this exact horizontal exposure and date. A 1,800 × 1,202 reproduction was downloaded unchanged. Its diagonal foreground walkway, central ladder cluster and front carriers match the smaller [Yancey Richardson](https://yanceyrichardson.com/artists/sebastiao-salgado) image. No frame from another part of the sequence is substituted.
- **Children lifting their empty cups, waiting to be fed, Vienna**, David Seymour (Chim), 1948. [ICP collection record BS1685](https://www.icp.org/browse/archive/objects/children-lifting-their-empty-cups-waiting-to-be-fed-vienna) identifies this exact image. The complete 690 × 700 museum scan includes narrow print edges. Resolution is limited; no artificial sharpening or upscaling was done. This is related to the Vienna feeding study saved in Notion, not an asserted match to the exact Notion exposure.

One original image per entry follows Julio’s updated photography instruction. Neither is licensed for public-app use by the act of downloading it for review. Copyright and collection credits are retained. Neither selection is approved.

## Salgado: meaning and source challenge

The pre-draft account linked the scale of manual labour to unequal roles within a gold economy. The prose needs the ownership/payment structure, because spectacular crowds alone can obscure it.

Evidence read:

- [Folha’s Portuguese report](https://arte.folha.uol.com.br/ilustrada/2017/sebastiao-salgado/serra-pelada/mina-de-ouro-parecia-formigueiro-caotico-mas-era-um-sistema-bem-organizado/), especially paragraphs following Salgado’s first testimony. Supports paragraph two. Later geological and present-day mining sections were not needed for the selected exposure.
- [Stallabrass, *Sebastião Salgado and Fine Art Photojournalism*](https://julianstallabrass.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/salgado.pdf), opening six PDF pages, with particular attention to pp. 1–3. Supports the modern-economy reading in the last paragraph. It also challenges interpretations that turn a documented situation into a timeless spectacle. Its exact pay-per-trip estimate was not repeated.
- [Bowdoin’s *People Watching* labels](https://www.bowdoin.edu/art-museum/pdf/final-people-watching-bowdoin-texts-labels-june28.pdf), printed page 9, identifies the same work and discusses labour exclusion. It describes 1986 as the final operating year. That does not agree with later activity recorded elsewhere; the draft therefore gives the photographic date without a mine-closure claim.
- [Reddit discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/interestingasfuck/comments/gmryhi/after_gold_was_discovered_in_a_stream_in_1979/) and [a second thread with replies](https://www.reddit.com/r/HistoricalCapsule/comments/1h3mp5j/surreal_photos_from_the_serra_pelada_gold_mine/) were inspected. Responses repeatedly mistake the scene for an earlier century. Replies disagree about slavery, self-employment and contemporary mining comparisons. These are leads for checking, not historical evidence or a measured consensus. The final account avoids calling every worker an owner or an enslaved person, and does not make unsupported comparisons to current electronics supply chains.

Observed directly: the supplied frame contains several scales of workers, vertical ladders and visible foreground loads. The eye-movement sentence is editorial interpretation of those visible elements, not a statement of Salgado’s declared intention. No participant is named because the selected sources do not identify them. The entry omits generic biography, awards and population-wide motives.

## Chim: meaning and source challenge

The pre-draft account centred continuing nutritional need after the fighting stopped, the practical organisation of relief, and the photograph’s use in an institutional project. It does not diagnose photographed children or infer private histories from their faces.

Evidence read:

- [ICP’s complete object record](https://www.icp.org/browse/archive/objects/children-lifting-their-empty-cups-waiting-to-be-fed-vienna) and its image fix the exposure, date and collection.
- [Magnum’s *Children of Europe*](https://www.magnumphotos.com/newsroom/society/david-seymour-children-of-europe/), full relevant essay and Vienna captions. Supports the programme described in paragraph two. The field caption about medical categories accompanies a related Vienna photograph, so the prose says “Chim’s captions” and does not assign a medical category to these individual children. The essay also includes disputed claims about Tereska, an unrelated photograph; those were not adopted.
- [E/ICEF/73 Add.1, 18 July 1948, Annex 1](https://www.cf-hst.net/unicef-temp/Doc-Repository/doc/doc323673.pdf), PDF page 14 / index 13. The digitised administrative record corroborates shared international and Austrian provision. OCR and indexed text were available; the web screenshot timed out. It records the country programme, not the identity of this school.
- [Carole Naggar’s substantive article](https://time.com/3796013/a-second-look-chims-children-of-war/), 16 January 2013. Read the actual article paragraphs after unrelated material the page now prepends. Its discussion of editorial selection keeps the account from reducing the entire project to needy children. The last paragraph draws on its description of the book’s sequence. Less than 100 words of the delivered text and notes derive from that article.
- A public discussion search did not produce a useful sustained discussion of this specific exposure. It did lead to scholarship on the project’s publication history. No reception consensus is claimed.

Observed directly: children hold cups up toward an elevated camera; expressions and gaze directions vary. The text does not say the scene was candid or staged, because no inspected record settles that. It does not call these children orphans, camp survivors or traumatised. The photograph shows a group at one moment; the relief documents supply broader context separately.
