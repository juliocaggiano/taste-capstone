Checked the opening frame, Antigone's appeal to unwritten divine law and the Creon–Haemon exchange in [Jebb's translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Sophocles/antigone.html). The final outcomes are understood through the essays but withheld from the entry. The first performance is approximate: UCL places it in the late 440s; metadata varies between 442 and 441 BCE. Use “around 441 BCE,” never an exact performance day.

**Reception and challenge.** Read the opening and selected substantial reply chains in the [part-one reading discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/AYearOfMythology/comments/1bxcbxf/antigone_part_one_lines_1_700_reading_discussion/). Readers disagree about order, religion and fear of appearing weak. Some speculate about succession and misdescribe the opening; these are not adopted. No comment scores were exposed. UCL also challenges an easy heroine/villain account, but does not make Creon's conduct equally justified.

**Selection.** The body connects burial, personal duty and authority. Haemon's nine-word sentence makes the ruler/city distinction concrete. Antigone's religious claim is explained beforehand, so the quotation does not turn the play into a purely secular protest story. Ismene supplies the fear and practical cost of resistance. No inference that Sophocles advocated modern individualism.

**Budget.** Jebb supplies plot/dialogue; UCL supplies contextual interpretation. Keep each below 200 derived words across entry and notes. No words are quoted from UCL or Reddit.
