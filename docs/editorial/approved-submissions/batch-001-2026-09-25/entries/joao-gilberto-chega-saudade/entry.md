# Chega de Saudade

João Gilberto; music by Antônio Carlos Jobim, lyrics by Vinicius de Moraes · 1958 recording

![Blue Odeon label for João Gilberto’s Chega de Saudade LP, MOFB 3073, listing Chega de Saudade as the first track.](assets/selected.svg)

In Chega de Saudade, a man misses his lover and asks for her return. He begins in sadness, then imagines the affection they could share if she came back. The music becomes brighter as his thoughts turn toward reunion. João Gilberto’s 1958 recording makes this change feel intimate, with a quiet voice that sounds close to ordinary speech.

The guitar keeps moving while Gilberto stretches a word or starts a phrase slightly before or after its beat. Voice and instrument remain together without matching every accent. He draws on samba’s dance rhythms, fitting the work of several percussion instruments into the patterns of one guitar. The singing can therefore sound unhurried while the accompaniment stays lively.

The song was written by Antônio Carlos Jobim and Vinicius de Moraes. Elizeth Cardoso had already recorded it, with Gilberto playing guitar, before he made his own version that July. Jobim arranged the instruments accompanying him. Released as a single in August, Gilberto’s recording became a landmark of bossa nova, the emerging Brazilian style associated with this close relationship between understated singing and rhythmic guitar.

Cardoso’s earlier recording was already part of this experiment. Working with Gilberto, she reduced the wavering effect called vibrato in parts of her singing. The new style developed through exchanges among performers, composers and arrangers, each changing how a familiar Brazilian rhythm could accompany a voice.
